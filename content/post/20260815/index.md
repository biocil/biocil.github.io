Maha Sahloul’s paper, [All-optical selective target highlighting for augmented reality microscopy](/publication/2026_optcon_sahloul/), written with Kaan Akşit of University College London, was selected for the cover of [Optics Continuum, Volume 5, Issue 8](https://opg.optica.org/optcon/issue.cfm?volume=5&issue=8) (15 August 2026).

## What the paper does

Augmented-reality microscopes usually highlight objects of interest with a camera, a computer and a display, which adds delay. Maha’s approach moves that processing into the optics. A phase-only spatial light modulator sits in a Fourier plane of the microscope and displays phase maps that were optimised offline with a physics-based model of the instrument. Once loaded, the maps highlight targets selected by size or shape directly in the live view, with no per-frame computation or tracking.

The highlighting follows targets as they move across the field of view and tolerates some rotation and partial occlusion. The current system is a dark-field proof of concept, and it points to a broader idea: doing part of a microscope’s image processing inside its optical path.
