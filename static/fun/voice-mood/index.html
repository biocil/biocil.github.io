How to read this, and what it cannot do
  • What is measured. A pretrained neural network (audEERING Wav2Small, trained on natural English podcast conversations) scores each 3 s of speech for activation, pleasantness and assertiveness. Pitch, speaking rate and pauses are measured directly from the signal. No words are recognised, so Turkish and English are handled the same way.
  • The mood label is mostly activation: Tired-sounding or Subdued (low), Calm, Animated, then Agitated, Excited or Angry-sounding (high). Activation is the dimension voices reveal most reliably and it carries across languages well; pleasantness is much weaker.
  • Angry versus excited sound alike. The label says Angry-sounding only when activation is high and both a low pleasantness score and a small classifier trained on acted English clips agree. Otherwise it says Agitated.
  • Tired-sounding is not a sleepiness measurement. The index combines low activation, flat pitch, slow speech and more pausing. It has not been validated against sleepiness ratings. Use the log on this page to collect ratings and check it for your speakers.
  • Personal baseline. People differ. Set my normal voice records the speaker's usual values so the labels mean "more or less than usual for this person" rather than "compared with a typical speaker".
  • One voice, close microphone. Everyone the microphone hears is treated as the same speaker. Keep the microphone within about half a metre of the person; background noise and distant speech push activation readings upward. The Signal line warns when the level is too low.
  • How a voice sounds is not how a person feels. Treat the output as an indication, and only use it with the consent of everyone being recorded.
